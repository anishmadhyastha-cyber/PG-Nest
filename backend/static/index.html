Recommendation Methodology & Mathematics

Learn how PGNest computes recommendation scores using Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA).

1. Non-Linear Priority Weight Scaling

When you rate your priorities on a scale of 1 to 10, a linear translation often dilutes extreme preferences. To solve this, PGNest applies a quadratic power-scaling function to the user-defined ratings:

$$w_j = \frac{p_j^2}{\sum_{k=1}^n p_k^2}$$

By squaring the ratings before normalization, a critical requirement (10/10) gets 4 times the weight of a moderate requirement (5/10). This ensures dominant criteria mathematically drive the rankings, matching realistic student trade-offs.

2. Criteria Normalization

Since criteria have different units (Rent in ₹, Distance in km, Safety in stars), they must be normalized to a dimensionless range [0, 1]:

Maximization Criteria (Safety, Quality, Amenities):
$$r_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij} - x_j^{\text{min}}}{x_j^{\text{max}} - x_j^{\text{min}}}$$
Minimization Criteria (Rent, Proximity Distance):
$$r_{ij} = 1 - \frac{x_{ij} - x_j^{\text{min}}}{x_j^{\text{max}} - x_j^{\text{min}}}$$

This maps the worst-performing property to 0.0 and the best-performing property to 1.0 for each specific criterion.

3. Weighted Sum Model (WSM)

The Weighted Sum Model is a linear, additive multi-objective aggregation method. The overall rating score for a PG accommodation i is calculated as:

$$S_i = \sum_{j=1}^n (w_j \cdot r_{ij})$$

It is highly intuitive and linear: a high score in one criterion can compensate for a low score in another.

4. TOPSIS Algorithm

The Technique for Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) is a non-linear geometric MCDA algorithm. It ranks accommodations based on their distance to the ideal positive and negative bounds:

  1. Vector Normalization: Renders criteria scale-independent.
    $$v_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij}}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^m x_{kj}^2}}$$
  2. Weighted Normalized Matrix: Multiplying weights: $$y_{ij} = w_j \cdot v_{ij}$$
  3. Identify Ideal Best ($A^*$) & Negative-Ideal Worst ($A^-$):
    Considers best and worst achievable values across the cohort.
  4. Compute Euclidean Distances to Ideal Points:
    $$S_i^* = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^n (y_{ij} - y_j^*)^2} \quad , \quad S_i^- = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^n (y_{ij} - y_j^-)^2}$$
  5. Compute Closeness Coefficient:
    $$C_i = \frac{S_i^-}{S_i^* + S_i^-}$$

TOPSIS favors accommodations that are balanced and penalizes those with extreme weaknesses.
